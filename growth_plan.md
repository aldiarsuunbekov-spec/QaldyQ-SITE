# QaldyQ — first 50 cafes

## Objective and measurement

Target 50 activated independent cafes, bakeries and small grocery shops: a provisional split of 25 in Almaty and 25 in Astana. Treat the split as a test allocation, not a claim about market size. Acquire by dense neighborhood clusters rather than spreading listings across each whole city.

Count a merchant as **activated** only after its first valid deal is published. Track **retained-active** separately: at least three new deal listings in a rolling seven-day period and at least one buyer reservation. The current MVP records reservations but does not confirm pickup/payment, so reservations are a proxy—not verified completed sales or food-waste reduction. Confirm outcomes with merchants manually until pickup verification is added.

## Positioning and offer

Lead with a practical promise: **“Turn tonight’s unsold food into incremental revenue, with no setup fee and a pickup-ready workflow that takes a few minutes.”** Explain the economics plainly: at the standard 20% commission, a completed ₸2,000 discounted-bundle sale yields ₸1,600 to the merchant and ₸400 gross commission to QaldyQ before payment, support and refund costs. Do not promise that every listing will sell.

Offer a no-fixed-fee, 14-day pilot with the standard 20% success-based commission clearly disclosed. Give each founding cafe concierge onboarding, a QR poster, a simple closing-time checklist and help creating the first three offers. Avoid changing the commission silently or subsidizing buyer discounts before contribution economics are known.

## Acquisition playbook

1. **Build a qualified lead list.** Identify 250 prospects—125 per city—with predictable evening surplus: independent bakeries, pastry shops, cafes with a display case, prepared-food counters and small groceries. Record venue, neighborhood, decision-maker, contact channel, typical closing time, likely surplus category and next action. Prioritize owners/managers who can test without a head-office approval cycle.
2. **Sell neighborhood density.** Choose one compact pickup cluster per city first, ideally around offices, campuses and residential foot traffic. Recruit supply within walking distance before broad consumer promotion. Do not onboard isolated venues merely to inflate the signup count.
3. **Use founder-led field sales.** Visit during quiet periods or shortly before close—not during the rush. Ask what is left on a typical weekday, how it is currently handled, and what a no-show costs them. Show the working cafe form and calculate proceeds from the merchant’s own bundle price. Follow up once with a specific pilot date, not repeated generic messages.
4. **Use warm distribution.** Ask food wholesalers, bakery-ingredient suppliers, POS/accounting implementers, coworking operators, campus administrators and local business communities for introductions. Send individually tailored Instagram or WhatsApp messages to public business contacts; respect opt-outs and avoid bulk unsolicited blasts.
5. **Make activation concierge-simple.** In one short session, create the merchant profile, publish three well-described offers, set an exact pickup window, and give staff a QR card linking to the storefront. Provide a copyable daily template for title, quantity, discounted price and pickup cutoff. Target first listing within 48 hours of the verbal yes.
6. **Turn successful pilots into referrals.** After a merchant has at least 20 buyer reservations confirmed manually with the venue, offer a one-time **₸3,000 QaldyQ fee credit** for a referred cafe that publishes its first 10 offers. At the planning assumption of ₸2,000 average sale price, 20 reservations produce ₸8,000 gross commission; the credit leaves ₸5,000 before variable costs. Recalculate once real basket size, pickup rate and support cost are measured.
7. **Create proof, not hype.** With consent, publish a short case study showing listings, reservations, pickup-confirmation proxy, merchant proceeds and kilograms reported by the cafe. Label pilot data and dates; never present seeded demo listings as partners or reservations as completed pickups.

## Funnel and six-week operating target

Planning funnel (hypotheses to measure, not established conversion rates): 250 qualified venues → 175 decision-makers reached → 100 two-way conversations → 65 pilot demonstrations → 50 activated merchants. If response or activation rates miss the targets, revise the segment, script or cluster before adding more outreach volume.

A founder or field seller should log daily: new prospects, decision-makers reached, demos, first listings, time-to-first-listing, deals per merchant, reservations per active merchant, reported pickups/no-shows and reasons for churn. Review by city and neighborhood every week; shift effort toward the cluster with higher activation and repeat listing, not the city with more raw leads.

## Unit economics and decision gates

Use explicit assumptions until pilot data replaces them. At an average discounted basket of **₸2,000**, each reservation creates **₸400 gross commission** if fulfilled. At **20 fulfilled orders per active cafe per month**, gross commission is **₸8,000 per cafe/month**. If all 50 cafes reached that rate, that would be **1,000 orders, ₸2,000,000 gross merchandise value and ₸400,000 gross commission per month**, before payment fees, refunds, support, taxes and acquisition costs. At an assumed average bundle weight of 0.5 kg, 1,000 verified pickups would represent 500 kg saved; do not report this from reservations alone.

Set a fully loaded acquisition-cost ceiling of **₸15,000 per activated cafe** as a pilot hypothesis. At ₸8,000 monthly gross commission per cafe, simple pre-cost payback would be about **1.9 months**; real payback will be longer after variable costs and should use fulfilled orders, not reservations. Pause paid acquisition if projected payback exceeds three months or if the merchant does not repeat-list within two weeks.

Product/market gates: first listing within 48 hours; at least 60% of activated cafes publish again in week two; at least 20 fulfilled orders per retained cafe/month as an initial target; cancellation/no-show rate tracked by cohort; and positive contribution after payment and support costs before scaling referral credits. Pickup verification and order status are product gaps to close before making verified waste-diversion or completed-sales claims.

## Copyable first-contact message

> Здравствуйте! Я [имя], запускаю QaldyQ — сервис, который помогает кафе продать часть вечерних остатков гостям поблизости. В пилоте нет абонплаты: стандартная комиссия — 20% с подтверждённого выкупа; самовывоз пока подтверждаем с заведением вручную. Поможем бесплатно настроить страницу и выложить первые три предложения. Можно показать демо за 10 минут и прикинуть экономику на ваших обычных остатках? Если неактуально — больше писать не буду.

Send individually to a public business contact, personalize one sentence about the venue, and respect a no or opt-out. Do not claim automated payment, pickup verification or guaranteed sales; those are not in the current MVP.

## Geodata onboarding and discovery funnel

During concierge onboarding, confirm the merchant's actual pickup address and place the map pin with the owner/staff member; never use a city-centre placeholder as the cafe's location. Verify the pin against the venue before the first offer goes live, and set a realistic pickup start/end window. As a pilot hypothesis, target geocoding at least 90% of active offers in each launch cluster by the end of week two; track the share of listings with verified coordinates, map/list view selection, time-filter use, listing-card-to-reservation conversion, and reservation rate within 25 km. Compare cohorts by city and neighborhood; keep city/search/list as a complete fallback for unlocated inventory. Treat map opens and localStorage bookings as product engagement signals, not completed pickup or validated willingness to pay.
